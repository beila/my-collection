One of the takeaways from this, and from the 2023 OpenAI saga, is that a firm's nominal governance structure isn't necessarily its real governance structure.

Note that I'm not making any judgement about whether Huang or Altman should have been fired. The point is that in both cases, the board members were nominally empowered to make that decision, and did make it, rightly or wrongly. But when the rubber hit the road, it turned out that there were other forces that overruled them.

This means that we should have some skepticism about governance mechanisms that haven't been tested, such as the one that Anthropic has set up. The idea that board members will actually be able to carry out decisions that have massive negative ramifications for major stakeholders turns out to be questionable in practice.
