Indeed. NFS:U2 came out in late Nov 2004, goes double platinum, sells 11M copies and EA shut down online multiplayer in Aug 2006. Reasonable debate can be had about time frames but games out of print over 20 years should be an easy call. The problem isn't just copyright duration, it's also lack of granularity. We need a middle-ground like 'Expanded Fair Use' which kicks in after 20 years and enables non-commercial sharing of the actual code, without also looping in rights to long-lived franchise characters (Mario, Sonic, Zelda) or an entire 'cinematic universe'. It's the 'all-or-nothing' that lets publishers effectively block everything by arguing "But then anyone will be able to make all-new Need For Speed or Final Fantasy games."

As for "all sorts of cool possibilities", just look at what the retro modding community has already done with HD texture packs, expansions and fan translations.

2026-11-07 · comment — Was able to get webGPU working without losing the sandbox (Sandboxed=true on chrome://gpu) by doing (agent output below):

1. Copy NVIDIA’s Vulkan manifest to a filename Chrome’s sandbox permits:

   sudo install -D -m 644 \
  /usr/share/vulkan/icd.d/nvidia_icd.x86_64.json \
  /etc/vulkan/icd.d/nvidia_icd.json
2. Launch Chrome with NVIDIA-only Vulkan enumeration and forced WebGPU interop:

   VK_DRIVER_FILES=/etc/vulkan/icd.d/nvidia_icd.json \
   google-chrome-stable \
  --ozone-platform=wayland \
  --enable-features=ForceEnableWebGpuInterop
And then now I just have my chrome launcher store that environment variable with the feature set via chrome://flags/#force-enable-webgpu-interop

(Note: running proprietary RPMFusion Nvidia drivers on Fedora KDE Wayland)
