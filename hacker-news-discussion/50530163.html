2026-11-17 · comment — The study cepr.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum9881/files/2026... finds a ≈0.01 standard-deviation difference in test scores the year lockable phone pouches are adopted, with no detectable additional difference in the following year. 2026-11-17 · comment — That ≈0.01 SD effect is actually 0.0087 +/- 0.0043 (standard error), giving a 95% confidence interval of roughly 0.0003 to 0.0171 standard deviations.

So the effect could be tiny.

Worse, the report says it excludes observations outside the 0.5th and 99.5th percentiles of the 2021–22 fall-to-spring score-change distribution. I'm not sure how this is defensible. Either you trust your measurement instrument or you don't.

If you're using a difference-in-differences design, excluding observations can change what you're measuring, particularly if the exclusions differ between treatment and control groups or over time. Without trimming those observations, the estimated effect drops from 0.0087 SD to 0.0058 SD (and is no longer statistically significant).

The authors preregistered their study, but there's nothing in the report indicating that the trimming of 'extreme' values was part of that preregistration...

2026-11-17 · comment — The identification in this study is a sketchy, and a similar study in the exact same setting ((tom-dee.github.io/files/w35132.pdf) doesn't find a significant effect.

1. they see diverging pre-trends, which immediately suggests a problem in the diff-in-diff 2. they're doing some kind of propensity score matching to build a comparison set, and it looks like they're matching across state lines, which is going to expose them to all sorts of weird state-level shocks 3. while the previous study just looked at the impacts on grades, they've built a value-added model and they're estimating their effects through that. That just adds another place where they can get the model wrong and end up with bad estimates.

They also point out that the schools that bought these pouches had more ESSER money, which they could have spent on tutoring.
