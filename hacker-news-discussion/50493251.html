2026-11-14 · comment — Reading this thread makes me realize that seemingly no one has heard the basic, mundane case for why Nvidia would want to do so much "circular financing" as a matter of totally normal business strategy. This may not be the whole story, but it's worth understanding.

Because of bottlenecks in the supply chain, Nvidia knows it is going to sell 100% of its inventory. They could raise prices quite a bit more, but then their only customers would be hyperscalers (building competing chips) and possibly OpenAI and Anthropic. Nvidia is willing to forgo present day profits for the strategic value of keeping its customer base diverse and promoting the network effects of e.g. CUDA. Instead of quoting a lower price per GPU to neoclouds and startups, they finance purchases of chips, but the two are roughly equivalent (financing means Nvidia takes on more risk, but has more upside and locks in future demand). It is in their commercial interest to have many competing buyers of their chips.

Obviously this creates fun mechanisms for fraud and wacky accounting (and I'm agnostic as to whether that's happening here), but it's worth knowing about the boring/obvious story before donning your tinfoil hat.

In general though, yes, I agree. Hyperscalers are trying to reduce dependence on NVIDIA. Many are quite far along in their ASIC projects (Google especially, they are on their 8th generation TPU! Way past just being a small scale experiment by now.) and have large volumes of them running in place of NVIDIA chips and much more efficiently already.

Broadcom is fabless, and uses TSMC for these sorts of chips. They are mostly a boutique design "mercenary" firm for exactly this sort of specialized hardware for Hyperscaler customers and other clients that run significant amounts of infrastructure in house (so, think JP Morgan for instance). That's the core of their business, along with increasingly Infrastructure Software in the form of virtualization and mainframes and private and hybrid cloud (so some natural sales overlap there, just generally, "big companies that need specialized big data center hardware and software").
