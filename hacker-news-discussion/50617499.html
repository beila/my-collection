The Lanham Act and common-law "naked licensing doctrine" requires companies to defend their trademark in order to keep it. And if they license it out, they are required to quality control how it is used, or they will lose their trademark.

If you simply let other people use your trademark, either implicitly or explicitly, you lose it.

They could license it out, but they'd have to do it in an official capacity and actively police the content on the site. It would honestly be more simple to just hire the guy.

2026-11-23 · comment
If the site is in the US:

The instruction set itself is not protectable IP; this has been thoroughly litigated. You can document it, use it, and implement it to your heart’s content, as long as (1) you didn’t get access to it under a contract that disallows disclosure (making it a trade secret), and (2) you don’t violate any patents when implementing it or using it.

If the site is using prose or diagrams from manuals, that prose and those diagrams are protectable IP covered by copyright. Mathematical descriptions of the instructions (equations), however, are not protectable because they’re considered purely functional and not creative.
