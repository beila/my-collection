There are some interesting conversations about how'd you ban open weight models and so far the emerging consensus is that if you can't ban the bullets, then you ban the guns.

Or you could just impose tort liability for individuals who misuse models, OSS or otherwise, and not ban anything.

I've never figured out why nearly all of the air in the "AI regulation" space is taken up with ex-ante regulatory proposals. All of the dominant approaches like the EU AI Act include some variation of getting experts to "audit" the weights for "bias", and risks, etc. and approve the models before release (which imposes costs only large corporations can pay) or outright caps/bans based on model size or specific capabilities. There's precious little active interest among regulators towards ex-post regulation, like statutory tort liability (which wouldn't impose onerous requirements on OSS developers, only users).

Note: there is decent academic work on ex-post regulatory approaches, but much, much less interest in ex-post stuff among those in the pro-regulatory community with influence.

The most important thing to discuss in advocating for open-weight models is how to address their safety concerns. Highlighting e.g. the obvious conflicts of interest that Dario has is not enough to convince people to ignore the safety risks. We need a concrete counterproposal for how open-source models can proceed while mitigating these risks to acceptable levels.

For example:

1. Supposing that at some point in the future, open-source models can find and exploit software vulnerabilities, how do we e.g. protect critical infrastructure around the world?

2. Supposing that at some point in the future, open-source models can walk a non-chemist through the process of making nerve gas, how do we prevent nerve agent attacks?

3. Supposing that at some point in the future, open-source models can help a non-biologist design a novel pathogen, how do we prevent the resulting pandemic?

These are not meant as rhetorical questions. They are real questions that need real answers. Personally I have no clue what the answer is, and would love to read some serious answers. I tried to order them from most to least imminent.
