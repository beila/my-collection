2026-11-22 · comment

Ford and GM have been doing this since 2002 with the GM–Ford 6-speed automatic transmission [0]

All the manufacturers have been doing it since even before that, with major items like engines and transmissions.

What they haven't been doing is standardising all those other parts that need replacing. For example, standardising on the wheel hub+knuckle for 15-inch, 16-inch, 17-inch and 18-inch wheels will let wheels of the specific size use the same disks, calipers, pads, balljoint, wheel bearing, CV joint and all the sensors (wheel speed, brake, etc).

I see no reason why two different SUVs on 17-inch wheels need completely different brake pads shapes, when both sets have the same surface area, require the same performance profile, etc, but that's what we have now.

You can do the same with almost every mechanical and electrical subsystem in a car, and still have a large variety in cars produced. After all, car manufacturers don't manufacture their own tyres and batteries, do they? They don't manufacture they own wheels.

Even the things that are specific to them, they outsource anyway - they don't each have their own windscreen manufacturer, they outsource that. The already outsource all the glass, all the oils and fluids, all the pipes, etc, it's jus that they produce incompatible designs so that the actual parts manufacturer has to charge a jig setup fee for each of them. Standardising on parts brings in economies of scale.
