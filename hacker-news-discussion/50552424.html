2026-11-18 · comment — Anthropic spent a lot more time editing their pre-training data with higher-quality material. For example, they used actual books for pre-training, because books are generally run through human editors for higher quality. (this is from the copyright lawsuits)

Other companies would use completely raw internet data for pre-training, which is basically trash. Anthropic's secret sauce is that they filtered and edited down their data for only the highest-quality material. You really don't need quadrillions of tokens to pre-train, as that's just random noise. A smaller-but-higher-quality data set is better in every way.

Meanwhile its obvious that companies like Google would use trash data like email and chat logs or Internet forums for pre-training. Just complete trash data.

This is a classic case of "less is more" and "garbage-in-equals-garbage-out"

2026-11-19 · comment — I'm terrible with names, so I'm in the same boat. There's a lot of marketing going on.

But the naming isn't completely random, here's what I use to make the ordered list in my head:

Claude is named after creative works, and uses bigger works for more powerful models. In order of strength:

* Haiku - short poem (we all know this one)

* Sonnet - "medium" length poem (many of us know this one)

* Opus - It's the singular form of "opera", so it's like a stand-alone segment of a larger body of work. (Nobody knew this one, but not hard to remember, and know you know that "opera" is actually plural! The description sounds bigger than a sonnet, but it's not a strict ordering.)

* Fable - a story (this one doesn't seem obviously bigger than an opus, but I guess an opus is one part and a fable is a front-to-end story, it works)

* Mythos - another type of story, which works and it's basically Fable but Anthropic's internal-only version

Gemini, as of version 4, started doing something with atoms:

* Hydrogen - smallest periodic element, the most common gas in outer space

* Neon - a bigger element, less common in outer space

* Argon - yet bigger element, yet less common in outer space (as best I can tell, I am not an astronomer)

* Carbon - this breaks that pattern, its a smaller element than the first three, and not a gas... but critical for life on this planet? And it sounds like a longer version of Argon. So I can remember it, but I'm not sure what the intended pattern was.

The model ordering is not strictly upgrades, but AFAIK they are intended to be steps up in terms of (claimed) complexity and reasoning power. But since it's an evolving field, the specifics of how cost, speed, and accuracy combine can make it hard to know how to order a given model for your use case. (i.e. cheap & fast with a harness that makes more calls, or expensive and slow but better at one-shot).

Deepseek's naming is more organized and literal, but you still have to remember the prefixes for V = version (plain) and R = Reasoning, and that they have different specialized models that aren't intended to be compared to each other.
