2026-11-20 · comment — These proofs really are quite huge. The proof is 1.3GB, ~15M lines of code:

  mkdir -p riemann_proof && cd riemann_proof

  # fetch parts 01–14
  for i in {01..14}; do
    curl -fSL -o lean.tar.gz.part$i https://alpo.ge/lean.tar.gz.part$i || { echo "failed: part$i"; break; }
  done

  # join and decompress (creates ./lean)
  cat lean.tar.gz.part{01..14} > lean.tar.gz
  tar xzf lean.tar.gz

  # size, file count, line counts
  du -sh lean | cut -f1 | xargs printf '%s on disk (lean folder)\n'
  find lean -name '*.lean' | wc -l | xargs printf '%s .lean files\n'
  find lean -name '*.lean' -print0 | xargs -0 cat | wc -l | xargs printf '%s lines total\n'
  find lean -name '*.lean' -print0 | xargs -0 cat | grep -cv '^[[:space:]]*$' | xargs printf '%s non-blank lines\n'

2026-11-20 · comment — In truly layman's terms, this is among the most famous problems in mathematics; it is arguably the holy grail of pure mathematics and number theory. It is also another one of the Clay Millennium Prize problems.

In the ~150 years since it was posed, it has become a lynchpin for hundreds of downstream results that assume it is true. This proof would immediately confirm all of those results.

More practically, there are also some algorithms that will now have guaranteed theoretical bounds and it will likely eventually lead to some more interesting algorithms in the future.

It's kinda the math equivalent of "we found life on another planet".
