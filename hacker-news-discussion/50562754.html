> they figured out how to make a program that does the job

No they figured out how to make software that makes the job easier. How is this any different from the introduction of optimizing compilers or type systems or any of the other software tools we all use? It makes programmers more productive, which means programmers can take on larger projects.

Coding agents have limits and the managers who forced their SWEs to use them are starting to figure that out. Using agents to both write code and review code sounds cool in theory but in practice it is not such a great idea -- it means that nobody actually understands the codebase and therefore nobody can actually debug the code when things break. No, you cannot just assume that you can also throw an agent at the problem when things break -- because the breakage might mean you lost access to the agent or the agent lost access to your system and cannot help you.

> difficult to grasp how much life will change in the coming years

Not too difficult to grasp. Shareholders will demand returns and Anthropic will have to deliver those returns. All the sky-high dreams about RSI and the next phase of human existence are going to evaporate and profit is going to be front and center.

2026-11-19 · comment — $2T is a pretty misleading number. You can't just add up all outstanding shares over the next N years and give them the same value as current ones. Not all of them will get vested. And ones that do will lower the price of existing shares (which is the "diluted" part of fully diluted market cap). A company doesn't become worth 20% more if it issues 20% new shares. Existing shares become worth less.

Anthropic hit a peak market cap of $1.46T post IPO and is now at $1.27T. It may go up to $2T over the next few years when more shares vest, or it may not. Sites reporting $2T today just want to maximize engagement ("we annouced a bigger number than the other guy!").
