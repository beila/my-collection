A friend and I have run relisten.net for ~13 years as a frontend to Archive's Live Music Archive (among other sources). We take great care to respect all parties involved and operate it completely non-commercially as FOSS.

Archive in general is very friendly to this sort of thing. Perhaps they feel differently about video, but they are very encouraging - assuming you act responsibly (self-identifying scraping, respecting rate limits, respecting licensing, and so on).

There's an old thread from 2009[0] that endorses this. Of course, please be respectful of the media and the network so that we may all continue to build wonderful experiences on top of Archive.

Archive walks the walk: they believe in their mission deeply and back it up (no pun intended).
[0] archive.org/post/261115/hotlinking-allowed

I've had fun building something similar using FieldStation42 (fieldstation42.com).

Runs on a RPI5 with external HD. Lives in an old cable box I gutted. Uses HDMI CEC so works with my TV remote.

Some media came from the Internet Archive, some ripped from old DVDs I've been saving in my attic. Commercials come from YouTube compilations and the Internet Archive.

This has scratched a serious itch for watching TV "how it used to be" before streaming. We sit down, watch a show at a set time, then go about our day/night. No temptation to binge. If we truly want to watch a show, we'll make time for it. If not then it wasn't a priority.
