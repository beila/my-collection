"Music for 18 Musicians" is perhaps Steve Reich's most popular composition, and because this orgy of syncopation is only an hour long, and because it's celebrating its 50th anniversary, and because Steve Reich is celebrating his 90th birthday, somebody had the brilliant idea to perform it three times by three different ensembles in the massive Drill Hall of the Park Avenue Armory.

First up was the Steve Reich Ensemble. At least two members of the current lineup have been around for half a century. This was the most sedantary, time-honored traditional rendition.

Following a little performance by Caroline Shaw that substituted for an intermission, we saw the Tactus Ensemble from the Manhattan School of Music. In this performance -- characterized by lots of young-musician energy -- the vocalists, string players, and clarinetists wandered around the stage area like troubadours, forming little mini ensembles to play for each other and for us.

The third performance was by the renowned new-music ensemble Alarm Will Sound, who also tended to wander around but in a more rehearsed way. During the maracas sequence of the composition, one of the vocalists persuaded people in floor seating to get up and start dancing, and then people in the elevated seats stepped down and joined them, until the whole stage area was filled with people dancing with the sheer ecstasy of the music, which didn't seem to phase the musicians at all. When the maracas stopped (as they suddenly do) there was some brief applause and the dancers dispersed back to their seats.

People dancing to the music of Steve Reich is a new one on me.

Not everyone made it to the end of this 3-1/2 hour extravaganza, but I heard more of my favorite Steve Reich composition in one sitting than I ever thought possible.
